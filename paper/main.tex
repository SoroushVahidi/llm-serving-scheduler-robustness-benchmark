\title[Portability of LLM-Serving Scheduler Rankings]{How Portable Are LLM-Serving Scheduler Rankings Across Workloads, Operating Regions, and Metrics?}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Soroush} \sur{Vahidi}}\email{sv96@njit.edu}

\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{Department of Computer Science, Ying Wu College of Computing}, \orgname{New Jersey Institute of Technology}, \orgaddress{\street{University Heights, GITC Suite 5700}, \city{Newark}, \postcode{07102}, \state{NJ}, \country{USA}}}

\abstract{Large language model (LLM) serving systems are typically compared on one
workload, operating region, and metric. We present the LLM-Serving Scheduler
Portability Benchmark (LSSP), a preregistered evaluation of scheduling
policies across three independently sourced workloads and six calibrated
operating regions ($18{,}720$ evaluations). Rankings agree strongly between
two of three sources (Kendall's $\tau$ up to $1.0$) but are less portable
when the third is involved (as low as $0.55$), with $3.6\%$ of comparisons
showing statistically supported reversals. Portability is even more
sensitive to the evaluation metric: median rank correlation across metrics
is only moderate ($\tau_b=0.419$), and the top policy agrees between metrics
in only half of conditions. Rankings are more stable, though not invariant,
under alternative service-level-objective definitions. A selected
physical-vLLM validation preserved a stable control but not a
simulator-predicted reversal. Scheduler comparisons should be read as
conditional on the workload, region, and metric that produced them.}
