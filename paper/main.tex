\title{How Portable Are LLM-Serving Scheduler Rankings Across Workloads,\\
  Operating Regions, and Metrics?}
\author{Soroush Vahidi}
\date{Draft V1 --- pre-Phase-12 authoritative consolidation, 2026-09-02}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Large language model (LLM) serving systems rely on sophisticated
scheduling mechanisms, evaluated on workloads that are themselves
heterogeneous across source, time, and operating region. We ask a
second-order question: is the \emph{comparative ranking} a scheduler
benchmark reports portable, formalized as $R(W, M, L)$, a comparative
scheduler ranking as $W$ (workload source/window), $M$ (metric), and $L$
(operating/load region) vary? We present the LLM-Serving Scheduler
Portability Benchmark (LSSP): a preregistered, paired evaluation spanning
three workload sources, 120 workload windows, a calibrated six-region
operating grid, an 11-primary/13-total scheduling-policy
panel, and 18{,}720 completed cells, plus a benchmark sample-complexity
study and a targeted real-vLLM validation design. We establish, as evidence
available now, that the three workload sources are measurably different
and strongly separable in their observed descriptor distributions, and, as
historical pilot evidence, that scheduler differentiation is real but
strongly source-dependent. LSSP's central results remain outstanding and
are stated as explicit, outcome-neutral result contracts.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} LLM inference serving; request scheduling;
ranking portability; performance evaluation; workload characterization;
benchmark reproducibility
