\title{How Portable Are LLM-Serving Scheduler Rankings Across Workloads,\\
  Operating Regions, and Metrics?}
\author{Soroush Vahidi}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Large language model (LLM) serving systems are typically compared on
one workload, operating region, and metric, but how portable is that
comparison? We present the LLM-Serving Scheduler Portability Benchmark
(LSSP), a preregistered evaluation of scheduling policies across three
independently sourced workloads and six calibrated operating regions
($18{,}720$ comparisons). Rankings agree strongly between two of three
sources (Kendall's $\tau$ up to $1.0$) but are less portable when the
third is involved (as low as $0.55$); $3.6\%$ of comparisons show
statistically supported reversals. Portability is even more sensitive
to the evaluation metric: median rank correlation across metrics is
only moderate ($\tau_b=0.419$), and the top policy agrees between
metrics in only half of conditions. Rankings are more stable, though
not invariant, under alternative service-level-objective definitions.
Real-system validation remains planned. Scheduler comparisons should
therefore be read as conditional on the workload, region, and metric
that produced them.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} LLM inference serving; request scheduling;
ranking portability; performance evaluation; workload characterization;
benchmark reproducibility
