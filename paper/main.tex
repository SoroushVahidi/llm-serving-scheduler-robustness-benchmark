\title{LLM-Serving Scheduler Portability Benchmark (LSSP):\\
  Does Comparative Scheduler Ranking Generalize Across Workload Sources,\\
  Operating Regions, and Metrics?}
\author{Soroush Vahidi}
\date{Draft V1 --- pre-Phase-12 authoritative consolidation, 2026-09-02}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Large language model (LLM) serving systems increasingly rely on
sophisticated scheduling mechanisms, and prior work has separately
established both that these mechanisms are numerous and mechanistically
diverse, and that the workloads used to evaluate them are themselves
heterogeneous across source, time, and operating region. We ask a
second-order question that follows from combining these observations: is
the \emph{comparative ranking} a scheduler benchmark reports itself
portable across independently sourced workloads, operating regions, and
evaluation metrics? We formalize this as the portability of $R(W, M, L)$, a
comparative scheduler ranking induced by a fixed policy panel under a fixed
benchmark protocol, as $W$ (workload source/window), $M$ (metric), and $L$
(operating/load region) vary. We present the LLM-Serving Scheduler
Portability Benchmark (LSSP): a preregistered, paired, multi-source
evaluation of 13 scheduling policies across three independently released
workload sources, a calibrated six-region operating grid, and an explicit
completion-conditioned metric contract. We establish, as evidence available
now, that the three workload sources are measurably different and strongly
separable in their observed descriptor distributions, and, as historical
pilot evidence, that scheduler differentiation is real but strongly
source-dependent. LSSP's central ranking-portability, sample-complexity,
and real-system-validation results are not yet available at the time of
this manuscript and are stated as explicit, outcome-neutral result
contracts rather than asserted in either direction.
\end{abstract}
