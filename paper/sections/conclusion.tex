\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have formulated the portability of a comparative scheduler ranking,
$R(W, M, L)$, as a benchmark object distinct from both workload
characterization and single-scheduler evaluation, and we have designed,
frozen, and documented a paired, multi-source evaluation protocol -- a
mechanism-diverse fixed scheduler panel, a calibrated six-region operating
grid, an explicit metric contract, and an uncertainty/reversal-detection
methodology -- before generating any comparative outcome. We have
established, as project-local evidence available now, that the three
primary workload sources underlying this benchmark are measurably
different and strongly separable in their observed descriptor
distributions (\S\ref{sec:workloads}), and, as historical pilot evidence,
that scheduler differentiation under this project's protocol is real but
strongly source-dependent, motivating the larger panel and denser load
grid used going forward (\S\ref{sec:stage0}). The benchmark's central
questions -- whether comparative scheduler rankings are portable across
independent sources, operating regions, and metrics, how many workload
windows are required before such a ranking is trustworthy, and whether
simulated rankings reproduce on a real serving engine -- remain open at the
time of this manuscript and are stated as explicit result contracts
(\S\ref{sec:results}--\S\ref{sec:real-system}) rather than asserted in
either direction. We plan to release LSSP as a public, independently
reproducible artifact (\S\ref{sec:artifact}) once these results exist.
