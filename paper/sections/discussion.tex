\subsection{If Ranking Portability Is High}

If scheduler rankings prove largely portable across the three independently
sourced workload families, operating regions, and metrics tested
(\S\ref{sec:cross-source-rankings}--\S\ref{sec:metric-conditioning}), the
practical implication is that future scheduler comparisons may not need to
be repeated across many independently sourced workloads to be trustworthy:
a smaller, well-chosen evaluation footprint could suffice, reducing the
evidence burden of scheduler evaluation. A high-portability result would
also constitute evidence of a kind of robustness for the panel's
mechanisms themselves -- that their relative strengths and weaknesses are
not artifacts of any one workload's specific structure. At the same time, a
high-portability finding on this benchmark's three sources and fixed
operating-region grid would not license an unconditional claim of
portability to every workload a practitioner might encounter; the boundary
conditions under which portability was observed (source identity, load
range, metric set, and simulator fidelity; \S\ref{sec:threats}) would need
to be stated precisely, and \S\ref{sec:sample-complexity}'s result would
still bound how much evidence is required before a specific portability
claim is statistically warranted.

\subsection{If Ranking Portability Is Low}

If scheduler rankings instead prove fragile -- with practically meaningful,
statistically supported reversals concentrated in particular sources,
regions, or metrics (\S\ref{sec:reversals}) -- the practical implication is
the opposite: single-workload scheduler evaluations risk over-generalizing,
and a comparative claim of the form ``scheduler $A$ outperforms scheduler
$B$'' should be read as conditional on the workload, metric, and load
region it was measured under, unless independently re-verified. This would
motivate multi-source evaluation as a de facto requirement for
general-purpose scheduler comparisons going forward, and would sharpen the
importance of \S\ref{sec:sample-complexity}'s sample-complexity result for
practitioners who cannot afford to test every candidate workload
distribution. A low-portability finding would also raise the practical
question of which workload characteristics predict a reversal
(RQ4, \S\ref{sec:cross-source-diffs}, \S\ref{sec:telemetry}) -- explanatory,
not selector-building, in scope (\S\ref{sec:evidence-boundaries}).

\subsection{Mechanism Equivalence and Collapse}

Independent of which branch above applies at the ranking-portability level,
the mechanism telemetry collected for every cell (\S\ref{sec:telemetry})
allows a finer-grained discussion of \emph{why} rankings behave as observed
-- for example, whether policies that appear to tie on a given workload do
so because their scheduling decisions genuinely converge (as Stage~0's
historical diagnostic found for several policies on BurstGPT's
structurally short-prompt traffic; \S\ref{sec:stage0}) or because the
metric used cannot distinguish otherwise-different decision sequences. This
discussion is deferred to the post-Pilot-V2 revision of this section, once
\S\ref{sec:cross-source-rankings}--\S\ref{sec:metric-conditioning} exist to
anchor it.

\subsection{Workload Descriptor Interpretation}

The workload-separability result (\S\ref{sec:separability}) establishes
that the three sources are observably different, and that prompt-length
structure is the primary driver of that separability
(\S\ref{sec:drivers}). Whatever RQ4's descriptor-to-reversal association
analysis finds, it should be read against this backdrop: an association
between a descriptor and a reversal does not by itself establish that the
descriptor is a genuine \emph{causal} driver of the reversal, only that it
is a source-separating feature that co-occurs with where reversals were or
were not observed.

\subsection{Benchmark Construction and Practical Reproducibility}

Regardless of outcome direction, this study's paired design, fixed
scheduler panel, and preregistered protocol (\S\ref{sec:study-design})
are intended to make the benchmark itself reusable: a future scheduler
design can be dropped into the same panel and evaluated against the same
frozen windows, operating regions, and metric contract, using the planned
public artifact (\S\ref{sec:artifact}) rather than re-deriving the protocol
from this manuscript's prose.
