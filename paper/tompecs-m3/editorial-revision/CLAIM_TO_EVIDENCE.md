## Consequences for novelty

The prior draft repeated the headline effects and model exclusions without making the closest predecessor comparisons explicit. The revision leads with one linked argument: determine executable distinctions, measure the residual performance problem, then check whether ranking conclusions survive redundant comparisons. The reduced analysis adds a concrete saved-evidence result, not just stronger rhetoric.

This materially strengthens the case, but does not turn the four-to-three collapse into a general scheduling theorem. Its proof is straightforward once the contract is written down. The main empirical depth remains one restrictive contract. A skeptical full-journal referee could reasonably ask whether the diagnostic teaches more than this case. The smallest scientifically meaningful extension would be a targeted, prereasoned contract perturbation on matched saved inputs: make token work binding (with common timing otherwise fixed), predict the first point at which priority order can change selected spans, and verify that boundary and its performance consequences. Composition-dependent timing is a separate alternative, not a requirement to run a new broad campaign. **Neither extension was run or claimed in this task.**
