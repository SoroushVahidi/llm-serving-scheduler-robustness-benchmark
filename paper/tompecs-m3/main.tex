\title[Scheduler Rankings Under a Common Model]{Scheduler Rankings and Performance Trade-offs in a Common-Model LLM-Serving Evaluation}
\author{Soroush Vahidi}
\email{sv96@njit.edu}
\affiliation{\institution{New Jersey Institute of Technology}\city{Newark}\state{New Jersey}\country{USA}}
\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
A scheduler ranking is a property of an evaluation contract as well as a
workload. Comparing implementations with different service progression,
resource enforcement or policy information can obscure what a ranking
measures. We evaluate four project-maintained LLM-serving scheduler
adaptations under one explicit CPU discrete-event model. The complete
finite-corpus design contains 120 windows from three trace sources, six
inherited arrival/deadline settings and two KV reservation capacities:
5,760 validated cells, including 144 reused pilot cells. All requests
complete. Across six source/capacity aggregates, the SLO-gated adaptation
reduces mean latency by 11.3--34.0\% relative to chunked-FCFS while reducing
token throughput by 1.65--4.01\%; BurstGPT's mean window-level p95 latency
increases at both capacities. The urgency gate never binds, so these gains
do not demonstrate urgency-aware scheduling. Chunked-FCFS and decode-first
are exactly equivalent in all 1,440 matched conditions. There are no
strict 10\% practical reversals between source means, although window-level
and metric-dependent reversals occur. A separate 134,000-study known-truth
diagnostic identifies calibration concerns in historical inference
procedures; it does not confer statistical guarantees on trace comparisons.
The contribution is an auditable model-based evaluation showing how
absolute trade-offs, structural equivalences and aggregation jointly
constrain ranking claims, rather than a new scheduler or a native-GPU
performance prediction.
\end{abstract}
