\section{Related Work and Evaluation Scope}
\paragraph{Evaluation methodology and the closest comparison.}
Papaioannou and Doudali study how datasets and inference scenarios change
LLM-system evaluations \citep{papaioannou2024workload}. Agrawal et al. identify
anti-patterns in baseline comparisons, evaluation settings, and metric
design \citep{agrawal2025evaluation}; Etalon introduces a fluidity index to
capture streaming experience missed by conventional summaries
\citep{agrawal2024etalon}. These works already establish that benchmark and
metric choices matter. Our study adds a different object of analysis: executable
behavior under shared timing, resource, and information rules. It derives
an exact equivalence for two adaptations, verifies it over the complete
matched matrix, and measures the trade-offs left after that equivalence.
The source-mean/window contrast then shows how aggregation changes the
interpretation of those surviving comparisons. This is a contract-specific
case study, not a general evaluation methodology or a new streaming metric.

\paragraph{Serving and scheduling mechanisms.}
Orca introduces iteration-level scheduling \citep{yu2022orca}; vLLM's
PagedAttention addresses KV fragmentation and sharing \citep{kwon2023vllm};
Sarathi-Serve combines chunked prefill with stall-free batching
\citep{agrawal2024sarathi}. Fu et al. learn relative output-length order to
approximate shortest-job-first scheduling \citep{fu2024ranking}. Our
prompt-length ordering uses observed inputs, without predicting output
length or claiming the same objective or optimality.
Bari et al. distinguish the throughput-optimal RAD construction from
practical SLAI, which combines decode urgency and known prompt lengths
\citep{slai2025}. SOLA jointly considers request/system state and work
allocation to improve SLO attainment \citep{hong2025sola}. Their active
state-dependent mechanisms differ from the fixed, inactive urgency gate.
The published systems establish the value of their mechanisms under their
own execution assumptions; they do not establish this adaptation panel's
state equivalence. Full-context reservations also omit native paging, while
disaggregation and migration, exemplified by DistServe and Llumnix
\citep{zhong2024distserve,sun2024llumnix}, lie outside the evaluated contract.

\paragraph{Binding constraints and throughput.}
Dai et al.'s May 18, 2026 revision studies queueing stability with token-load-
dependent batch time and a maximal-request-batch-size extension
\citep{dai2026throughput}. The binding constraint matters in both studies,
but the estimands differ. Our contract fixes batch time, limits residency to four or
eight requests, and measures output divided by finite-window completion
duration. Its original-contract chunked work cannot exhaust the token budget;
Section~\ref{sec:budget} separately tests lower budgets. These drained
rates are not a stability region, and the observed throughput losses neither
refute Dai et al.'s results nor establish a new optimality theorem.

\paragraph{Workloads and simulators.}
BurstGPT and ServeGen characterize production-derived workloads
\citep{burstgpt2025,servegen2026}; resampling has a longer history in scheduler
evaluation \citep{zakay2014resampling}. Our analysis instead conditions on a fixed
selected corpus, with equal-window source means and explicit matched
comparisons. Vidur combines profiling and prediction
\citep{vidur2024}; LLMServingSim and its heterogeneous/disaggregated successor
model hardware/software interactions \citep{llmservingsim2024,llmservingsim2026}.
They pursue hardware-aware performance estimation. Our fixed-duration
work ledger pursues checkable execution semantics. Following simulation
verification/validation terminology \citep{sargent2013}, journal
reconstruction verifies conformance to that model; physical prediction
would require separate validation.

\paragraph{Inference and evidence reuse.}
STAT01 applies established BH, BY, Holm, and concentration methods
\citep{benjamini1995,benjamini2001dependency,holm1979,hoeffding1963}.
Multiplicity correction does not validate input p-values or turn a
zero-effect test into a practical-effect test. The diagnostic investigates
those distinctions under specified synthetic truth. This study reuses historical
windows, reconstruction, and archived rule outputs, with separate execution
identities for the common-model comparison (Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}).
Shared infrastructure and related mechanism studies do not provide
independent validation of its results.
