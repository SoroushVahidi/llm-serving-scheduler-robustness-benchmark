\section{Related Work and Evaluation Scope}
\paragraph{Simulation and benchmark comparisons.}
Vidur combines operator profiling and predictive modeling with hierarchical
scheduling; its Vidur-Bench suite supplies workloads, scheduling/batching
policies, and performance metrics, while Vidur-Search explores deployment
configurations \citep[Sections~4--6]{vidur2024}. Beyond these established comparisons, we ask which policy distinctions
the shared model preserves: Section~\ref{sec:contract} proves reachable-state
equivalence for fixed policies, and Section~\ref{sec:urgency} bounds mechanism activation.
LLMServingSim and its heterogeneous/disaggregated successor also model
hardware/software interactions \citep{llmservingsim2024,llmservingsim2026}.
Our fixed-duration work ledger instead permits direct reasoning about
execution semantics. Journal reconstruction verifies conformance to that
model; physical prediction would require separate validation \citep{sargent2013}.

\paragraph{Evaluation methodology and metrics.}
Papaioannou and Doudali study how datasets and inference scenarios change
LLM-system evaluations \citep{papaioannou2024workload}. Agrawal et al. identify
anti-patterns in baseline comparisons, evaluation settings, and metric
design, with a speculative-decoding case study
\citep[Sections~3--4]{agrawal2025evaluation}; Etalon introduces a fluidity index to
capture streaming experience missed by conventional summaries
\citep[Sections~3--5]{agrawal2024etalon}. We adopt their caution about
benchmark and metric choices. Section~\ref{sec:rankings} adds a matched
account of source-mean, window-level, and cross-metric preferences after
demonstrated redundancies are removed. Its absence of source-mean practical
reversals has a specified threshold and denominator; it does not establish
universal portability. We introduce neither a new streaming metric nor a
general evaluation checklist.

\paragraph{Serving and scheduling mechanisms.}
Orca introduces iteration-level scheduling \citep{yu2022orca}; vLLM's
PagedAttention addresses key--value (KV) cache fragmentation and sharing \citep{kwon2023vllm};
Sarathi-Serve combines chunked prefill with stall-free batching
\citep{agrawal2024sarathi}. Fu et al. learn relative output-length order to
approximate shortest-job-first scheduling \citep{fu2024ranking}. Our
prompt-length ordering uses observed inputs, without predicting output
length or claiming the same objective or optimality.
Bari et al. give a formal batching framework and distinguish the
throughput-optimal construction from practical SLO-Aware LLM Inference
(SLAI), which combines
decode urgency and known prompt lengths
\citep{slai2025}. SOLA jointly considers request/system state and work
allocation to improve SLO attainment \citep{hong2025sola}. These systems evaluate active state-dependent mechanisms under their own
execution assumptions; our adaptation has a fixed, inactive urgency gate. Full-context reservations also omit native paging, while
disaggregation and migration, exemplified by DistServe and Llumnix
\citep{zhong2024distserve,sun2024llumnix}, lie outside the evaluated contract.

\paragraph{Length information and application objectives.}
FastServe uses known input length to initialize a skip-join multilevel
feedback queue, with output-token preemption and GPU--host state movement
\citep{wu2026fastserve}. In contrast, our residents cannot be preempted;
known prompt length changes only waiting admission order in SLO-gated.
PLA-Serve, called LAPS in its published PDF, separates long and short
prefills and combines queue separation with batching and execution
optimizations \citep{she2026plaserve}. Its prompt-length awareness is
therefore not equivalent to sorting one waiting list under constant batch
cost. These systems optimize measured serving performance; our question
is which differences remain executable after the contract is fixed.

JITServe uses imprecise request information refined during generation to
allocate service toward heterogeneous application objectives and maximize
goodput \citep{zhang2026jitserve}. Its active objective-aware decisions
contrast with our hidden metric deadlines and fixed token-gap gate.
Our inactivity certificate concerns this particular adaptation, not a
failure of objective-aware scheduling in JITServe or SLAI. Comparing their
reported gains directly with ours would conflate information, resource,
and execution assumptions.

\paragraph{Binding constraints and throughput.}
Dai et al.'s May 18, 2026 revision studies queueing stability with token-load-
dependent batch time and a maximal-request-batch-size extension
\citep{dai2026throughput}. The binding constraint matters in both studies,
but the estimands differ. Our contract fixes batch time, limits residency to four or
eight requests, and measures output divided by finite-window completion
duration. Its original-contract chunked work cannot exhaust the token budget;
Section~\ref{sec:budget} separately tests lower budgets. These drained
rates are not a stability region, and the observed throughput losses neither
refute Dai et al.'s results nor establish a new optimality theorem.

Ao et al. model endogenous memory growth and linear iteration times,
using a fluid benchmark to derive WAIT for known output lengths and Nested
WAIT for unknown lengths \citep{ao2026fluid}. Their stability and asymptotic
results are conditioned on memory assumptions; evaluation includes profiled
simulation and supplemental GPU evidence. Our full-context reservations
instead remove memory-growth admission risk, and our theorem characterizes
behavioral equality rather than throughput optimality. The finite drained
rates reported here cannot establish or refute those stability claims.

\paragraph{Workload scope.}
BurstGPT and ServeGen characterize production-derived workloads
\citep{burstgpt2025,servegen2026}; resampling has a longer history in scheduler
evaluation \citep{zakay2014resampling}. Our analysis instead conditions on a fixed
selected corpus, with equal-window source means and explicit matched
comparisons. This supplies evidence within those windows, not a new workload
generator or an estimate of production-wide scheduler preferences.

\paragraph{Inference and evidence reuse.}
STAT01 diagnoses established inference procedures
\citep{benjamini1995,benjamini2001dependency,holm1979,hoeffding1963};
it introduces no new method. Historical inputs and rule outputs are reused,
with separate common-model execution identities (Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}).
Shared infrastructure does not constitute independent validation.
