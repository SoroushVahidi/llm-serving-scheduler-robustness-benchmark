\section{Historical Comparisons and Inferential Reliability}\label{sec:inference}
\subsection{What the historical comparison establishes}
The archived LSSP campaign evaluated 13 implementations, with an 11-policy
primary panel, across 120 windows and six settings. Its 9,360 configurations
have two deterministic verification passes, not independent stochastic
replicates. Historical policy paths differed in prefill work, resource
enforcement and available information. Consequently, the preserved outcomes
are comparisons of policy--model combinations, not the four CM-0.1
adaptations. Numerical reproducibility cannot remove that distinction.

Of 990 historical primary pairwise comparison records, 36 meet the saved
point-margin and nominal-interval rule, spanning five policy pairs. We
retain that number as an \emph{archived-rule count}. It is neither 36
independent discoveries nor 36 reproduced common-model reversals. The
historical pipeline screened point estimates for practical differences,
formed bootstrap tail fractions around zero, adjusted a selected family
and required nominal percentile intervals to exclude zero. A noisy point
screen at 10\% followed by a zero-effect test is not a calibrated test
that the population effect exceeds 10\%.

\subsection{A separate known-truth diagnostic}
STAT01 generates independent synthetic studies with three sources and
eleven policies, normally 40 windows per source. Its 165 source-pair/policy-pair
hypotheses distinguish sign reversals from strict population practical
reversals. Eighteen scenarios vary ties, effect size, serial dependence,
variance, innovation distribution and missingness. The campaign completed
134,000 studies, retaining 112,275 existing studies and generating 21,725
missing ones. All original records remain intact. Twelve scenarios reached
the 8,000-study ceiling without all precision targets; the other six met
the original precision criterion. Completion is not calibration success.

There are three numerical interpretations. The eight original implementations
retain their frozen floating-point contracts. An added legacy-source
comparator reproduces the actual historical BH code. Eight further
comparisons use exact rational multiplicity thresholds: finite-bootstrap
probabilities on their count grids and analytic probabilities interpreted
as their represented binary64 values. The nine additions are explicitly
post-diagnosis. Their expanded uncertainty family is kept separate from
the original eight-method family. Appendix~\ref{app:stat} gives definitions,
stopping rules, all original scenario classifications and the numerical
boundary case; the supplement includes every endpoint of all 17 methods.

The actual historical-source comparator estimates practical FDR at
0.09025 under the IID equal null, with expanded-family Monte Carlo bounds
$[0.06878,0.11172]$. Under independent-block and AR nulls it rises to
0.91800 and 0.980375 (Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}). Its ordinary fixed-contrast
interval coverage is 0.647625 and 0.564875 in those two dependent scenarios.
Full-family BH reduces discovery error but does not repair the component
intervals: original full-BH practical FDR remains 0.51875 and 0.778625.
The largest final endpoint-mean difference between legacy source and the
frozen port is only 0.00025. Exact threshold arithmetic likewise leaves
the principal calibration concerns intact.

There is no universally certified replacement in these results. Analytic
procedures retain their stated Gaussian IID, independent-cluster or known-covariance
justifications, but their stringent joint empirical-compatibility screens
are generally inconclusive in nondegenerate assumption-valid scenarios.
That is not evidence against a mathematical guarantee under its assumptions.
The covariance oracle is not a deployable solution when covariance is
unknown. Grouped-bootstrap BY has ordinary-interval coverage 0.88775 even
in the IID equal null. Bounded Hoeffding-Holm has zero observed FDR and
100\% diagnostic coverage in its two bounded IID scenarios, but practical
power is zero at 40 windows and only 0.052632 at 400. Conservatism and
inconclusiveness are substantive findings, not reasons to change the rules.

\paragraph{Answer to RQ3.}
Historical arithmetic can be reproduced without establishing either a
shared execution comparison or calibrated inference. The synthetic cases
exhibit concrete failures of the historical discovery procedure; arithmetic
corrections do not resolve its target, dependence or selection problems.
Conversely, the synthetic generators do not identify which individual
historical trace conclusions are false. The descriptive common-model
results stand on their finite-corpus accounting and need no transferred
FDR guarantee.
