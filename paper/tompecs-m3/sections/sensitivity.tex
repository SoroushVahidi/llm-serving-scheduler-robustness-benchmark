\subsection{A prospective token-budget sensitivity}\label{sec:budget}
Before the reachable-state proof, we prospectively predicted that a smaller
batch budget would separate chunked-FCFS from decode-first by allowing
prefills to displace resident decodes. The unchanged engine requires every
whole prompt to fit the budget. We therefore selected the first three
BurstGPT windows in lexical order with maximum prompts above 512 and at
most 1,024 tokens: \texttt{w16}, \texttt{w23}, and \texttt{w28}.
All 200 requests, seeds, timing, and reservation rules remain fixed. The
72 cells combine these windows, LOW/HIGH\_PRESSURE, eight slots, budgets
32,768/2,048/1,024, and four adaptations; the 12 pilot cells are reused.
Every cell passes the event referee and completes all requests; the 24
baseline cells reproduce their archived outcomes.

The prediction is rejected: all 12 perturbed chunked/decode-first matches
retain identical normalized journals and terminal states. Theorem~\ref{thm:equivalence}
now explains why truncation can affect only the youngest resident prefill.
The perturbation nevertheless changes execution. At budget 1,024,
SLO-gated omits 42 of 35,976 ready-decode opportunities, with maximum anchor
age 2\,ms. Equation~\ref{eq:urgency-bound} gives respective 38, 51, and
44\,ms upper bounds, below the 495\,ms gate. Independent reconstruction
finds no activation in any of its 18 cells. No strict 10\% reversal occurs
in 576 eligible budget comparisons, and all throughput changes are below
0.4\% in magnitude. Complete effects and diagnostics remain in the
supplement. These selected-window observations check conformance; the
fixed-duration theorem supplies the broader equivalence result only under
its stated assumptions.

\subsection{Composition-dependent timing sensitivity}\label{sec:timing}
TIME01 changes batch progression to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:composition-time}
 T(P_b,D_b)=\left(0.5+0.5\frac{P_b}{512}+0.5\frac{D_b}{8}\right)\mathrm{ms},
\end{equation}
where $P_b$ and $D_b$ are executed prefill and decode tokens after resource
constraints. Arrivals within a batch become eligible at its actual end;
completion and anchor updates use that end. Equation~\ref{eq:composition-time}
is a synthetic timing function, not hardware-calibrated.
The study pairs 384 variable-duration executions with existing fixed-duration
results: eight deterministically selected windows per source, two settings
(LOW/HIGH\_PRESSURE), two capacities, and four adaptations. Recursive
stratification by mean prompt length, mean output length, and simultaneous
arrival fraction selects a median window identifier per bin. This is
purposive descriptor coverage, not a probability sample or evidence of
blinding to prior outcomes; settings are not recalibrated for equal utilization.

All 96 chunked-FCFS/decode-first pairs retain exactly equal normalized event
trajectories, timestamps, and terminal metrics. In this nonbinding-budget
design, common selected work has the same order-invariant duration and
hence the same successor state. This explains the observed coupling
without extending the fixed-duration Theorem~\ref{thm:equivalence}.
Table~\ref{tab:time-sensitivity} separates timing effects from policy contrasts.
Using the strict loser-relative $>10\%$ rule of Section~\ref{sec:reversal-rule},
there are no timing reversals in 792 eligible source-mean comparisons.
At window level, two of 6,309 eligible comparisons reverse (27 zero-denominator
exclusions). Both compare whole-FCFS with SLO-gated in BurstGPT window
\texttt{w30}, LOW, eight slots: p95 latency and p95 TTFT. They are correlated
tail endpoints in one condition, not independent replications. Absence of
strict reversals does not imply identical rankings.
