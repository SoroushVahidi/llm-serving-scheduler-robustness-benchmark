\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{From policy labels to interpretable comparisons.}
The structural analysis identifies which decision rules can change execution
before interpreting their rankings. The resident-prefix invariant and common batch-end
updates erase the chunked/decode-first priority distinction, even under
binding budgets; admission
competition and whole-prefill/decode exclusion survive. The urgency
certificate supplies a different kind of result: a bound on reachable
anchor age rules out an otherwise implemented branch. Together these
explain why a nominally richer policy panel need not represent more
executed mechanisms.

ABL01 identifies prompt ordering as the source of the residual
latency--throughput--deadline trade-off within the nonbinding contract.
This is a total intervention effect, not a decomposition of admission,
queueing, and batch-count mediators or a hardware-level causal claim. The saved
BurstGPT timeline illustrates how many earlier completions can lower mean
latency while a later final completion extends the drain. It does not
establish optimality or a corpus-wide causal decomposition. Source means
answer an equal-window average question; window reversals expose variation
concealed by that average, while cross-metric reversals concern different
objectives on the same workload. Their coexistence, even in the reduced
analysis, shows why stable aggregate preferences cannot select a scheduler
for every window and objective. Exact top sets further distinguish ties
from strict preferences.

\paragraph{Model adequacy and transfer.}
Fixed batch timing and conservative full-context reservations permit the
state-transition argument but omit composition-dependent efficiency,
kernel, bandwidth, communication, and native allocation costs. Theorem~\ref{thm:equivalence} permits binding budgets but depends on fixed
policies, an empty initial system, resident-first service, stable prefix
admission, and the specified progress rules. It does not establish
invariance under policy switching or arbitrary supplied states. The
budget sensitivity changes some admissions and SLO-gated decode service;
the finite-work certificate rules out urgency activation there. Longer
prompts fail the unchanged domain check at these smaller budgets.
TIME01 tests one synthetic composition-dependent timing function on
24 selected windows. It neither calibrates hardware costs nor establishes
robustness to arbitrary timing functions. ABL01's control coupling is
restricted to the original nonbinding budget and admission semantics;
it does not automatically extend to binding-budget SLO-gated execution.
Historical GPU studies do not validate M3's exact comparisons. Conformance checks establish implementation correctness
for the abstraction, not physical prediction accuracy \citep{sargent2013}.

\paragraph{Corpus and inference limits.}
The selected windows are finite and temporally related, and the Azure-linked
sources are not independent provider populations. Nonoverlap inside the C01
panel does not settle overlap with older cross-project BurstGPT selections.
Uniform priorities and synthetic output-derived deadlines restrict goodput
interpretation; the inherited transformation changes deadlines and arrivals
together. C01 consequently reports descriptive finite-corpus effects.
The timing subset makes this limit concrete: on all forty BurstGPT windows
at the same two settings, fixed-duration SLO-gated improves mean latency
by 15.24\% and 11.99\% at four/eight slots. The eight selected windows
instead worsen it under both clocks (Section~\ref{sec:timing}). Input
composition differences are descriptive, not an identified explanation;
neither subset nor full-corpus averages estimate an unmeasured population.
The reduced analysis is post hoc and removes only demonstrated redundancies;
its remaining metric families and overlapping comparisons are still dependent.

STAT01's known truth applies only to its generators. Post-diagnosis methods,
finite study counts, conditional interval availability, and conservative
power remain explicit in Appendix~\ref{app:stat}. Reproducible arithmetic
and valid population inference are distinct requirements. The extensive
checks reduce implementation and reporting risk without adding scientific
novelty or resolving model adequacy by themselves.

\section{Conclusion}
An admission-and-progress invariant proves selected-work equivalence of
fixed chunked-FCFS and decode-first adaptations from an empty serving
system under the declared contract, including binding budgets. Common
batch-end updates extend this equality to normalized transitions and
terminal outcomes. A separate finite-work certificate explains urgency
inactivity in the evaluated fixed-duration regimes. The controlled ablation
attributes the remaining trade-offs to prompt ordering within the original
contract. Synthetic timing sensitivity preserves observed pair equivalence
but reveals window-sensitive policy effects. Source-mean practical stability
coexists with local and cross-metric reversals after demonstrated
redundancies are removed. These results connect model constraints,
effective behavior, and the interpretation of performance comparisons;
they establish neither scheduler optimality nor hardware performance.
