\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{What is learned from controlling execution semantics?}
The useful comparison unit is an executable behavior, not a policy name.
Here that distinction has a concrete consequence: nominal priority changes
can leave every request outcome unchanged while admission competition still
changes the completion distribution. Section~\ref{sec:contract} establishes
the structural conditions; Sections~\ref{sec:performance} and
\ref{sec:rankings} quantify the remaining trade-offs. The post hoc reduction
shows that the source-mean/window contrast survives removal of duplicated
comparisons. These results go beyond a warning about unfair settings by
identifying exactly which distinctions disappear in this implemented
contract and what remains measurable.

The competing objectives themselves are established scheduling ideas.
The BurstGPT example contributes a traceable instance: many earlier
completions lower the sum of latencies even as a delayed final completion
extends the drain. It illustrates compatibility of the measured objectives,
not optimality of prompt-length ordering or a corpus-wide causal effect.
Similarly, source means answer an equal-window average question; window
reversals describe variation concealed by that average. Neither summary
supersedes the other, and exact top sets preserve information lost by
arbitrary tie-breaking.

\paragraph{Model adequacy and transfer.}
Fixed batch timing removes composition-dependent cost, nonbinding token
work exposes the role of admission, and conservative reservations provide
a known residency constraint without output-length prediction. Together
these choices support a precise analysis of state-transition distinctions.
They omit kernel, bandwidth, communication, native allocation, and changing
batch-efficiency costs. The proof's conditions delimit transfer: a binding
work budget or composition-dependent timing can invalidate its argument,
and loss of the nonbinding bound need not break equivalence. The focused
budget sensitivity retains it while changing some admissions and decode
service; budget saturation is rare in that short-prompt BurstGPT stratum.
Longer prompts fail the unchanged common-domain check at these budgets,
and composition-dependent timing remains untested. Independent
conformance checks establish implementation correctness for the abstraction,
not physical prediction accuracy \citep{sargent2013}. The findings provide
a comparison diagnostic to apply before interpreting a ranking, rather than
a deployment recommendation.

\paragraph{Corpus and inference limits.}
The selected windows are finite and temporally related, and the Azure-linked
sources are not independent provider populations. Nonoverlap inside the C01
panel does not settle overlap with older cross-project BurstGPT selections.
Uniform priorities and synthetic output-derived deadlines restrict goodput
interpretation; the inherited transformation changes deadlines and arrivals
together. C01 consequently reports descriptive finite-corpus effects.
The reduced analysis is post hoc and removes only demonstrated redundancies;
its remaining metric families and overlapping comparisons are still dependent.

STAT01's known truth applies only to its generators. Post-diagnosis methods,
finite study counts, conditional interval availability, and conservative
power remain explicit in Appendix~\ref{app:stat}. Reproducible arithmetic
and valid population inference are distinct requirements. The extensive
checks reduce implementation and reporting risk without adding scientific
novelty or resolving model adequacy by themselves.

\section{Conclusion}
This common-contract comparison links a structural reduction of scheduler
labels to a smaller, interpretable performance problem. Two adaptations
have identical terminal outcomes despite potentially different journal
ordering; an inactive urgency gate leaves admission ordering as an active
distinction. The surviving behaviors exhibit measured mean/tail/throughput
trade-offs. A prospective budget-only sensitivity retains the equivalent
pair despite small execution changes on three feasible BurstGPT windows.
Source-mean practical stability coexists with window and metric
reversals even after duplicated comparisons are removed. These bounded
findings make explicit what the evaluator permits a scheduling choice to
change. The supporting historical diagnostic separately shows why exact
arithmetic reproduction is insufficient evidence of statistical calibration.
