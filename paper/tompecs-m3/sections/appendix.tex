\section{Statistical Diagnostic: Definitions and Complete Scenario Summary}\label{app:stat}
\subsection{Study construction and truth}
A study draws a latent array $Y\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times n\times11}$, with
positive population means $\mu_{sj}$. Sources are independent within this
synthetic design. Dependence across policies is
$C(\rho)=(1-\rho)I+\rho\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}$, usually with
$\rho=0.5$. Temporal covariance is identity, independent five-window blocks
with within-block correlation 0.7, or AR(1) with correlation $0.7^{|i-j|}$.
Source-specific innovations have mean zero and variance one; a Cholesky
transformation imposes the specified covariance, with default scale
$\sigma=0.15$. The exact-ties scenario uses $\rho=1$.

Each family comprises $\binom{3}{2}\binom{11}{2}=165$ hypotheses.
For a source pair $(s,t)$ and policy pair $(a,b)$, sign-reversal truth is
$(\mu_{sa}-\mu_{sb})(\mu_{ta}-\mu_{tb})<0$. Practical-reversal truth is
\begin{align}
 &(\mu_{sa}>1.1\mu_{sb}\ \land\ \mu_{tb}>1.1\mu_{ta})\nonumber\\
 &\qquad\lor\ (\mu_{sb}>1.1\mu_{sa}\ \land\ \mu_{ta}>1.1\mu_{tb}).
\end{align}
Equality at 10\% is null. Truth is enumerated from decimal/rational mean
specifications, independently of estimated means and test decisions.

Equal-null means are 0.5. Stable and near-tie means are $0.5+0.01j$ and
$0.5+0.0002j$, respectively. A reversal of size $\delta$ assigns source 0
policies 0/1 means $0.5\pm\delta/2$, swaps them in source 1, and sets all
other means to 0.5. Small, medium, and large IID reversal scenarios use
$\delta=0.03,0.10,0.30$. The boundary scenario uses 0.55/0.50 and the reverse.
Block, AR, unequal-variance, mixed-distribution, and MCAR reversal scenarios
use $\delta=0.15$. Unequal scales are $(0.08,0.25,0.15)$; mixed innovations
are standardized normal, Student-$t_5$, and lognormal. MCAR retains each
policy/window observation independently with probability 0.8. Selective
missingness removes source 0/policy 0 and source 1/policy 1 observations
below their respective latent means; truth remains the unconditional mean.
Observed values cannot identify that target merely by ignoring missingness.

The bounded scenarios use IID standardized uniform innovations,
$\sigma=0.08$, and $\delta=0.30$, giving scores in $[0,1]$ analytically,
without clipping. All scenarios have 40 windows per source except the last,
which has 400. Raw arrays and masks are retained in the full artifact.
The 112,275 reused and 21,725 subsequently generated studies sum to the
completed 134,000-study design; no study was removed because of a numerical
disagreement between implementations.

\subsection{Frozen methods and post-diagnosis additions}
All methods use nominal level $\alpha=q=0.05$.
The historical screened procedure uses 2,000 bootstrap draws of matched
observed per-window policy differences, percentile intervals, and
$p=\min\{1,2\min[\Pr_{\rm boot}(\bar D\leq0),\Pr_{\rm boot}(\bar D\geq0)]\}$.
It combines source-side p-values by their maximum, applies BH to the
point-screened family, and requires both intervals to exclude zero.
The full-family variant assigns untested hypotheses p-value one and applies
BH over all 165. The historical plus-one BY variant transforms the
component bootstrap p-value as $\min\{1,(2000p+2)/2001\}$ before source
combination and applies BY over 165. These modifications do not make
selection or the original p-values valid by definition.

The analytic practical-effect methods use paired contrasts
$Z=Y_a-1.1Y_b$ and their reversed orientation. Each orientation combines the
two one-sided source tests by a maximum (an intersection--union test).
The union over orientations uses $\min\{1,2\min(p_1,p_2)\}$ before Holm
correction over 165 hypotheses \citep{holm1979}.
IID t-Holm uses paired Student tests; cluster t-Holm uses independent
five-window cluster averages, retaining only completely observed clusters.
Fewer than two complete clusters causes abstention. Their finite-sample
Gaussian justifications apply to their respective independent sampling
units with complete or MCAR observations, not arbitrary temporal dependence.
The covariance-oracle normal variant uses the known Gaussian covariance
of each observed matched sample mean. It does not access missing latent
outcomes and is not deployable without that covariance information.

Grouped-bootstrap BY resamples nonoverlapping five-window groups jointly
across policies, preserving masks and pairing. Its centered one-sided
component p-value is
$(1+\#\{\bar Z^*-\bar Z\geq\bar Z\})/2001$.
A zero-observation resample causes component abstention; it is not silently
excluded. The same orientation construction is followed by full-family BY.
No finite-sample guarantee was prespecified for this procedure. Bounded
Hoeffding--Holm applies only to the bounded IID scenarios. Since
$Z\in[-1.1,1]$, its component p-value is
$\exp[-2m\max(\bar Z,0)^2/2.1^2]$ \citep{hoeffding1963}, followed by the same
orientation and Holm construction. It abstains outside that domain.

The eight original identities are retained unchanged. The ninth method,
\path{legacy_source_screened_BH}, reproduces the actual frozen
historical source's arithmetic. Eight \texttt{exact\_} counterparts of the
original methods use explicit rational critical thresholds and exact
bootstrap-count grids. Analytic p-values are treated as the exact dyadic
values represented by their finite floats, not as infinitely accurate
analytic probabilities. Rational harmonic constants define BY thresholds.
These additions change numerical semantics, not the sampling assumptions,
screening target, or statistical validity of a component test.

\subsection{Coverage, availability, power, and Monte Carlo accounting}
For each independent study, let $R$ be discoveries and $V$ false discoveries.
False-discovery proportion is $V/\max(R,1)$; its expectation is FDR.
Familywise error is $\mathbf{1}\{V>0\}$, and power is true discoveries
relative to the number of alternatives when alternatives exist. Both
practical and sign truth are retained. Ordinary interval coverage uses a
fixed source 0/policy 0-minus-policy 1 contrast. For historical methods,
that interval is generated in a separate unconditional diagnostic stream.
Coverage of actually selected intervals is a different, selection-conditional
endpoint; it is not replaced by ordinary coverage.

Interval availability is reported separately. In the MCAR and selectively
missing scenarios, cluster-t ordinary coverage is based on 1,676 and 202
of 8,000 studies, respectively. Historical selected-interval coverage in
the IID equal null is 0.123834 over 958 studies emitting selected intervals,
whereas ordinary fixed-contrast coverage is 0.941875 over 8,000 studies.
These are study-level averages, not independent interval observations.
The complete endpoint CSV also includes null rejection fractions,
maximizer-set coverage and size, and deterministic-ID correctness; exact
empirical maximizing sets are not confidence sets.

The five possible analysis looks are 500, 1,000, 2,000, 4,000, and 8,000
studies per scenario. For a bounded study-level endpoint with sample variance
$s^2$ and available count $N$, the two-sided empirical Bernstein half-width is
\begin{equation}
 b=\sqrt{\frac{2s^2\log(4/\delta)}{N}}
 +\frac{7\log(4/\delta)}{3(N-1)}.
\end{equation}
The bounds clip $\bar X\pm b$ to $[0,1]$; empty endpoints are unestimated
with the full range. The construction uses the empirical Bernstein bound
and a finite union over both tails and all reported looks
\citep{maurer2009}. Conditional coverage uses the count of IID studies
emitting an interval, with that availability explicit.
The original allocation is $\delta=0.05/(18\cdot8\cdot16\cdot5)$;
the expanded report uses $0.05/(18\cdot17\cdot16\cdot5)$.
Dependence among methods and endpoints does not invalidate the union bound.
The wider accounting does not erase post-diagnosis method selection.

Stopping uses only the original eight methods. Required half-widths are
0.025 for practical FDR, familywise error, and null rejection fraction;
0.03 for ordinary interval coverage; and 0.08 for defined power.
Selected-interval coverage is not a stopping target.
The calibration screen flags concern if the practical-FDR lower bound
exceeds 0.05 or ordinary-coverage upper bound is below 0.95. Empirical
compatibility with tolerance requires FDR upper bound at most 0.06 and
coverage lower bound at least 0.94 in relevant assumption-valid scenarios.
All other estimable cases are inconclusive. The screen permits conservative
overcoverage and is not a proof of exact nominal calibration.

\subsection{Numerical boundary case and substantive limitations}
The saved scenario 11, replicate 6628, comparison index 47 has a screened
family of 43 entries. At rank 43 the stored p-value is 0.05. The original
port evaluates $0.05\times43/43$ as 0.049999999999999996 and does not reject;
the actual source evaluates $(43/43)\times0.05$ as 0.05 and rejects.
The mathematical bootstrap-grid comparison is $1/20\leq1/20$ and rejects.
The old referee still reproduces its mismatch on this preserved record;
the recovery contracts explicitly distinguish all three identities.

The reuse audit found 31 port-versus-source differences among the original
112,275 studies. At final aggregation, the largest difference in endpoint
means between those implementations is 0.00025. Only the exact screened-BH
addition changes final endpoint means relative to its original counterpart,
with maximum change $8.56\times10^{-7}$; the other seven exact variants'
endpoint means match their originals. Aggregate equality does not establish
identity of every rejection vector.

These small arithmetic differences do not repair the larger statistical
problems. Full-family historical BH has practical FDR 0.51875 in the block
null and 0.778625 in the AR null. Grouped-bootstrap BY has ordinary coverage
0.88775 in the IID equal null and 0.759875 in the AR null. Under AR,
cluster-t coverage is 0.8600 in the null and 0.864375 in the reversal
scenario because fixed five-window clusters remain dependent. Conversely,
low FDR can coexist with negligible power: in the block-reversal scenario,
cluster-t practical power is approximately 0.00000658, oracle-normal
0.000296, and grouped-bootstrap zero. Bounded Hoeffding power is zero at
$n=40$ and 0.052632 at $n=400$, with original-family bounds approximately
$[0,0.00401]$ and $[0.04462,0.06065]$. Inconclusiveness, abstention, and
conservatism remain findings rather than reasons to select a new reporting
threshold.
