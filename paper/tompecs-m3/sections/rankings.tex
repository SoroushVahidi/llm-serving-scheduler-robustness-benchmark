\section{Rankings, Aggregation, and Metric Choice (RQ2)}\label{sec:rankings}
\subsection{Stable practical source comparisons and heterogeneous windows}
Table~\ref{tab:reversals} reports the strict-10\% reversal rule with eligible
and total denominators. Across all source-mean comparisons the count is
\textbf{zero out of 2,376}. Each source mean uses all 40 windows at a fixed
setting, capacity, and metric. The denominator is
$3$ source pairs $\times6$ settings $\times2$ capacities $\times11$ endpoints
$\times6$ policy pairs. The common-model study thus does not reproduce a
broad claim of practical source-mean ranking reversals.

This null result is compatible with smaller sign changes and different tie
sets. Among 396 comparisons of source pairs at fixed settings, capacities,
and metrics, exact top
sets are equal in 307 and intersect in 323; complete average-rank vectors
are equal in 247. Identical practical ordering and identical full rankings
are different statements.

Window-level variation remains visible. Within-source window comparisons
yield 34,394 reversals among 1,847,700 eligible comparisons; Cartesian
cross-source window comparisons yield 109,913 among 3,788,640. For \anwg\
alone, the corresponding counts are 8,472/162,900 and 35,352/332,640.
The larger window-pair denominators arise from comparing every unordered
pair of 40 windows within a source (780) or every Cartesian pair across
sources (1,600). They are not larger independent sample sizes. These
summaries cannot establish that one axis causally contributes more
instability than another.

Within the same window, settings produce 920/236,853 eligible reversals,
capacity changes 350/47,418, and metric choice 11,795/473,580. The last count
compares policy-pair directions across endpoints; it demonstrates that a
preference can depend on the objective even with the workload and resources
fixed. It is distinct from comparing top sets, which asks whether at least
one best policy is shared rather than whether a particular pair reverses.

\subsection{Ties and redundant endpoints}
Table~\ref{tab:ties} retains exact ties instead of breaking them by policy
identifier. A unique mean-latency winner is not necessarily a unique
throughput winner: SLO-gated is uniquely best in 1,146 and 482 conditions,
respectively. Completion has a four-way tie everywhere. Treating an
arbitrary deterministic tie-break as a scientific winner would discard
that information.

\centering\small

conditions. ``Near-tie change'' counts conditions whose top set changes

is missing. ``SLO unique top'' always uses exact equality.}
\label{tab:ties}\input{tables/ties}
\end{table}

Several endpoints provide the same ranking mechanically. All requests finish,
so emitted-token and completed-token throughput are identical. At a fixed
condition, request throughput differs from token throughput only by a
workload-specific multiplier shared by every policy. All three throughput
rankings therefore coincide. Counting them as independent evidence would
exaggerate the breadth of a finding.

TPOT supplies a different warning. Chunked-FCFS, decode-first, and SLO-gated
produce essentially one output per millisecond once decoding begins.
The largest chunked-versus-SLO difference in cell mean TPOT is
$1.80\times10^{-16}$\,s. Floating-point timestamp subtraction can still
change an exact winner. The near-tie sensitivity changes mean-TPOT top sets
in 1,129/1,440 conditions and p95-TPOT top sets in 104/1,440. These differences
carry no practical performance significance. The original exact values
remain preserved, alongside the predeclared sensitivity.

Across all 79,200 metric-pair ranking comparisons, exact top sets are equal
in 27,915 and intersect in 57,602. These counts include redundant throughput
endpoints, completion ties, and numerical TPOT distinctions. They summarize
reporting consequences; they are not eleven independent tests of a
scheduler's robustness.

\subsection{Chronological summaries}
Figure~\ref{fig:chronology} reports SLO-minus-chunked \anwg\ differences in
four consecutive ten-window blocks. Across settings and capacities, block
means range from $-0.0120$ to $+0.2030$ for Azure, $-0.0300$ to $+0.2330$
for Bailian, and $+0.0150$ to $+0.3275$ for BurstGPT. Negative differences
occur in 8/48 Azure and 24/48 Bailian block/setting/capacity combinations,
and none of the 48 BurstGPT combinations. The frozen EARLY/MIDDLE/LATE
summaries show the same qualitative contrast: negative differences in
6/36, 20/36, and 0/36 combinations, respectively. These are corpus-order
sensitivities, not estimates of a temporal process or guarantees for future
traffic.

\paragraph{Answer to RQ2.}
No source-mean policy comparison reverses by more than 10\% in both
directions. Window-level and metric-dependent reversals nevertheless occur.
Exact ties, duplicated throughput rankings, and negligible TPOT differences
show why absolute magnitudes and top sets are needed alongside ranks.
Aggregation changes the question being answered; stability at the source-mean
level does not imply uniform preferences across its constituent windows.
