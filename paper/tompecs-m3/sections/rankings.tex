\section{Rankings, Aggregation, and Metric Choice (RQ2)}\label{sec:rankings}
\subsection{Stable practical source comparisons and heterogeneous windows}
Table~\ref{tab:reversals} reports the strict-10\% reversal rule with eligible
and total denominators. Across all source-mean comparisons the count is
\textbf{zero out of 2,376}. Each source mean uses all 40 windows at a fixed
setting, capacity, and metric. The denominator is
$3$ source pairs $\times6$ settings $\times2$ capacities $\times11$ endpoints
$\times6$ policy pairs. For this equal-window estimand, changing source does not overturn a
policy-pair preference by more than the declared margin in both directions.
The 2,376 comparisons include the equivalent chunked/decode-first pair and
redundant throughput endpoints; they do not constitute 2,376 independent
confirmations or establish population equivalence.

This null result is compatible with smaller sign changes and different tie
sets. Among 396 comparisons of source pairs at fixed settings, capacities,
and metrics, exact top
sets are equal in 307 and intersect in 323; complete average-rank vectors
are equal in 247. Absence of a strict practical reversal is weaker than equality of rankings
or top sets.

Window-level variation remains visible. Within-source window comparisons
yield 34,394 reversals among 1,847,700 eligible comparisons; Cartesian
cross-source window comparisons yield 109,913 among 3,788,640. For \anwg\
alone, the corresponding counts are 8,472/162,900 and 35,352/332,640.
The larger window-pair denominators arise from comparing every unordered
pair of 40 windows within a source (780) or every Cartesian pair across
sources (1,600). They are not larger independent sample sizes. These
summaries cannot establish that one axis causally contributes more
instability than another.

Within the same window, settings produce 920/236,853 eligible reversals,
capacity changes 350/47,418, and metric choice 11,795/473,580. The last count
compares policy-pair directions across endpoints; it demonstrates that a
preference can depend on the objective even with the workload and resources
fixed. It is distinct from comparing top sets, which asks whether at least
one best policy is shared rather than whether a particular pair reverses.

\subsection{Ties and redundant endpoints}
Table~\ref{tab:ties} retains exact ties instead of breaking them by policy
identifier. SLO-gated is uniquely best for mean latency in 1,146 conditions and for
throughput in 482. Completion has a four-way tie everywhere.

\label{tab:ties}\input{tables/ties}
\end{table}

All requests finish, making emitted-token and completed-token throughput
identical. Request throughput differs only by a workload-specific multiplier
shared by all policies at a fixed condition. The three throughput rankings
therefore coincide. Chunked-FCFS, decode-first, and SLO-gated also produce
essentially one output per millisecond after decoding begins: the largest
chunked-versus-SLO mean-TPOT difference is $1.80\times10^{-16}$\,s.
Timestamp subtraction nevertheless changes exact top sets; the predeclared
near-tie tolerance changes mean-TPOT top sets in 1,129 conditions and p95-TPOT
top sets in 104. These tiny distinctions have no practical performance meaning.

Across all 79,200 metric-pair ranking comparisons, exact top sets are equal
in 27,915 and intersect in 57,602. These counts include redundant throughput
endpoints, completion ties, and numerical TPOT distinctions. They summarize
reporting consequences; they are not eleven independent tests of a
scheduler's robustness.

\subsection{Post hoc view of distinct behaviors and metric families}
To test whether duplication drives the qualitative ranking conclusions,
we project the saved outputs onto whole-FCFS, chunked-FCFS, and SLO-gated.
We remove the universally tied completion endpoint and retain token
throughput as the representative of the three identical throughput rankings.
The resulting eight endpoints cover five families: latency, TTFT, TPOT,
throughput, and deadline attainment. Both mean and p95 remain for the three
time families because they describe different distributional features.
TPOT remains informative for whole-FCFS: its mean and p95 exceed the best
value by the same strict-10\% practical margin in 341 and 1,027 conditions,
respectively. Removing TPOT altogether would discard that contrast.

This view contains 11,520 rankings, 34,560 matched pairwise records, and
8,208 reversal groups. Independent exact-boundary calculations from absolute
metrics reproduce every retained archived group. Table~\ref{tab:reduced}
still has no source-mean practical reversals ($0/864$), but has window and
metric reversals. The qualitative contrast therefore survives removal of
these demonstrated redundancies. Rates change with the denominator;
the remaining endpoints and overlapping comparisons are not independent
replications. The original four-label, eleven-endpoint outputs remain intact.
Chronological sensitivities appear in Appendix~\ref{app:chronology}.

\paragraph{Answer to RQ2.}
Source-mean practical stability does not imply uniform preferences across
windows or objectives. This remains true after collapsing duplicate labels
and endpoints; magnitudes and top sets distinguish meaningful preferences
from ties and numerical noise.
