\section{Conclusion}
The complete common-model evaluation gives three bounded answers.
First, the four adaptation identities exhibit measurable performance
trade-offs under shared work and reservation accounting: SLO-gated improves
aggregate mean latency but loses throughput and can worsen tails or
goodput. Second, source-mean practical stability coexists with window and
metric heterogeneity, many exact ties and a structural equivalence between
two adaptations. Third, the historical and known-truth audits show why
reproducible arithmetic cannot substitute for a common execution contract
or calibrated inference.

These results support a model-based performance-evaluation contribution
with explicit comparison identities and complete finite-corpus evidence.
They do not establish native GPU rankings or a universally preferred
scheduler. Reporting the absolute costs, invariant cases and unfavorable
outcomes alongside ranking changes is essential to the resulting systems
interpretation.
