\section{Research Questions and Portability Measures}\label{sec:study-design}\label{sec:rq}
We organize the manuscript around four questions that the frozen outcomes
can address. This is a retrospective presentation change, not a new
preregistration. ``Answer'' below means a conditional description of the
specified implementations and corpus, not a population or native-system
claim.
\begin{description}
\item[Q1: Source and region portability.] How much do aggregate policy
orderings agree across the three released sources and six operating regions?
\item[Q2: Practical pairwise reversals.] Which policy pairs change order
with more than the frozen 10\% relative margin on both sides, and how
sensitive is their saved interval support to the analysis assumptions?
\item[Q3: Objective sensitivity.] How do metric choice, tied optima,
observation support and the saved alternative SLO definitions change
preferred-policy conclusions?
\item[Q4: Temporal and finite-reference stability.] How stable are
rankings across the available temporal splits and subsets of the
40-window source reference?
\end{description}

\subsection{Comparison object and statistical unit}\label{sec:unit}
Let $R(W,M,L)$ denote the ordering of a fixed policy panel under source or
window set $W$, metric $M$, and operating region $L$. Collapse the two
identical deterministic passes before aggregation. Policies share windows
within a source/region; different sources are not paired request by request.
A whole window is the historical resampling unit. This preserves
within-window policy dependence but assumes exchangeability between
windows; non-overlap alone does not establish that assumption.

For defined outcomes $y_{w,p,m}$, the historical aggregate is
\begin{equation}
 \bar y_{p,m}=|\mathcal W_{p,m}|^{-1}
 \sum_{w\in\mathcal W_{p,m}} y_{w,p,m},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal W_{p,m}$ contains the observed windows for policy $p$
and metric $m$. Empty support gives no aggregate. Pairwise difference
intervals instead use $\mathcal W_{a,m}\cap\mathcal W_{b,m}$. These
estimands coincide for complete ANWG observations, but need not coincide
for conditional metrics. Section~\ref{sec:results-cross-metric} examines
this distinction without imputing unobserved outcomes.

\subsection{Metrics}\label{sec:metrics}
The primary metric, Arrival-Normalized Weighted Goodput (ANWG), is
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{ANWG}=\frac{\sum_{i\in S} w_i}{\sum_{i\in R} w_i},
\end{equation}
where $R$ contains all arriving requests, $w_i\geq1$ is priority weight,
and $S$ contains requests completed at or before their deadline. Dropped,
unfinished and late requests retain denominator weight but receive no
numerator credit. Every window has arrivals. ANWG, completion fraction
and weighted completion fraction are therefore always defined.

The other eligible metrics are SLO-violation rate, weighted goodput, mean
and p95 latency, request and token throughput, and mean and p95 TTFT.
The first six require a completion; TTFT additionally requires a recorded
first-token time for a completed request. The executed ranking schema
does not contain p99 latency or TPOT. Undefined observations are omitted,
never assigned a zero, loss or tie. A condition can remain estimable even
when its policy means use different window subsets. Lower-is-better
metrics are negated for cross-metric comparisons.

Kendall tau-b preserves equal values as ties. With concordant and
discordant pair counts $C,D$ and one-sided tie counts $T_x,T_y$,
\begin{equation}
 \tau_b=\frac{C-D}{\sqrt{(C+D+T_x)(C+D+T_y)}}.
\end{equation}
A zero denominator is undefined. Top-$k$ and exact-order recovery instead
sort by metric value and then policy ID. Thus a single top choice need
not be a unique optimum. Q3 reports both the historical choice and a
post-hoc maximizing-set comparison.

\subsection{Saved uncertainty and reversal taxonomy}\label{sec:uncertainty}
The historical procedure draws 2,000 samples of windows with replacement
and records the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. We call these \emph{nominal
95\% resampling intervals}; their reproduction does not establish 95\%
coverage under temporal dependence or for discontinuous, tied rankings.
Undefined rank statistics are discarded before percentile calculation.
This occurs in 30 of 198 source comparisons; the two primary LOW
comparisons involving BurstGPT discard 10 and 12 of 2,000 draws.
The resulting intervals condition on an estimable resampled ranking,
even though all primary ANWG cell values are observed.
Cross-source ranking samples are drawn separately. Cross-metric ranking
samples share window indices. Cross-metric pairwise difference intervals
use separate seeded marginal draws for each metric, a distinct procedure
from the paired ranking bootstrap.

For policy pair $a,b$, the saved classification first compares separately
aggregated differences $d_x=\bar y_{a,x}-\bar y_{b,x}$ and $d_y$.
Opposite nonzero signs are required. Each absolute difference must exceed
10\% of the absolute value of the losing policy in that condition. A zero
loser makes this margin unestimable, except that an exact tie has zero
difference and margin. The threshold is a historical operational
convention, not an established deployment-utility cutoff or a confidence
bound on the effect size.

The five exclusive labels are: undefined/unestimable; stable (including
exact ties); microscopic sign change (at least one margin at most 10\%);
practical sign change with a saved interval including zero; and
\texttt{SUPPORTED\_PRACTICAL\_REVERSAL} (both margins pass and both
saved difference intervals exclude zero). We retain the final label only
as an artifact category, abbreviated \emph{saved-supported}. It does not
assert calibrated statistical significance. The sequential rule skips
interval calculation for stable and microscopic records; absence of the
wide-interval class does not establish equivalence between practical and
statistical criteria.

The saved tail quantity is
\begin{equation}
 p_s=\min\{1,2\min[\widehat P(d_s^*\leq0),
                         \widehat P(d_s^*\geq0)]\},\qquad
 p_{\mathrm{pair}}=\max(p_x,p_y).
\end{equation}
This is an empirical bootstrap tail fraction, not an exact binomial sign
test. The maximum has an intersection--union interpretation only if its
component tests are valid for the intended nulls. Here null calibration,
data-dependent directional screening and window dependence are not
established. Nor do zero-excluding intervals test the stronger hypothesis
that both population margins exceed 10\%.

The historical BH calculation uses only records reaching the interval
stage: families are metric/region for cross-source reversals and
source/region/metric-pair for cross-metric disagreements. Independence or
the required positive dependence of valid null $p$-values is not shown
\citep{benjamini2001dependency}. Consequently, the saved flags are
reproduced bookkeeping results, \emph{not a verified 5\% FDR guarantee},
even within families. Section~\ref{sec:audit-sensitivity} reports bounded
alternative bookkeeping and dependence sensitivities; none repairs null
calibration by changing a denominator alone.
