\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
LSSP treats the portability of a comparative scheduler ranking as an
explicit evaluation output. Its historical simulator corpus shows strong
but uneven cross-source agreement, a minority of practical reversals
spanning five policy pairs, and more limited agreement across metrics.
The contribution is the reproducible comparison design and the
conditional empirical characterization, not a novel rank statistic or a
universal scheduler recommendation.

These findings apply to the frozen implementations, windows, regions and
metric rules. Later audits narrow the native-system fidelity claims:
the legacy physical comparison cannot establish faithful scheduler-ranking
transfer, and CPU conformance is not physical execution evidence. Future
serving measurements require separately validated admission, execution,
KV, timing and output contracts before testing portability on hardware.
