# WORKLOAD_CHARACTERIZATION_PAPER_RESULT.md

Canonical, paper-facing workload-source-separability result. Supersedes
`source_classifier_metrics.json`/`feature_importance.csv` (the original,
random-split result — kept as historical evidence, not overwritten) for
any manuscript claim, per the findings of
`docs/SOURCE_SEPARABILITY_AUDIT_20260901.md`. Generated by
`scripts/characterization/paper_facing_separability.py`, read-only against
the frozen `window_descriptors.parquet` (2026-09-01 overnight run) — no
windows or descriptors were regenerated.

## What changed vs. the original result

1. **Feature matrix**: 28 features (`COMMON_NUMERIC_FEATURES` minus the 3
   deterministically redundant pressure proxies `approx_token_arrival_rate_tps`,
   `approx_concurrent_request_proxy`, `approx_kv_demand_proxy_tokens` — exact
   products of other included features, R²=1.0). They remain in the
   underlying descriptor dataset, just not in this classifier's input.
2. **Preprocessing**: median imputation + z-score standardization inside an
   `sklearn.Pipeline`, refit fresh on each fold's train rows only —
   structurally impossible to leak (regression-tested,
   `tests/test_separability_pipeline_no_leakage.py`).
3. **Evaluation**: leave-one-time_bucket-out (EARLY/MIDDLE/LATE) grouped,
   chronologically-blocked CV — not a random split.
4. **Permutation importance**: `n_repeats=30` (up from 20), computed on
   held-out grouped folds, properly aggregated across fold×repeat.

## Primary result (window_size=200, n=400 windows)

| Model | Balanced accuracy (pooled) | Macro-F1 (pooled) | Mean ± std across 3 folds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Random Forest** | **1.000** | 1.000 | 1.000 ± 0.000 |
| Logistic regression (multinomial) | 0.988 | 0.987 | 0.987 ± 0.007 |
| Decision tree, depth ≤ 3 | 0.980 | 0.980 | 0.980 ± 0.018 |

Per-fold detail (`grouped_model_metrics_per_fold.csv`): worst single fold
is the depth-3 tree with LATE held out (0.955); every other fold/model
combination is ≥ 0.977. Confusion matrices:
`grouped_confusion_matrices/{model}.csv`.

**Random Forest is the primary reported result.** The original random-split
RF (`results/.../source_classifier_metrics.json`, balanced_accuracy=1.0)
remains available as historical/contextual evidence that the random-split
and grouped-chronological evaluations agree.

## Safe claims

> "The four workload sources are readily distinguishable from their
> observed workload descriptor distributions, under a chronologically
> blocked (not random) train/test split."

> "Prompt/context-length structure accounts for much of this separability."

**Avoid** stating that "the providers intrinsically have different prompt
distributions" without the caveat below — some of the separation reflects
differing conventions for what "prompt tokens" means per source, not
necessarily a difference in the underlying conversations/tasks alone.

## Attribution: what drives the separation

`attribution_summary.csv` triangulates four independent signals (held-out
permutation importance, single-feature balanced accuracy, leave-one-
feature-out delta, feature-group-ablation delta), all computed under the
same grouped chronological CV. Two tiers emerge clearly:

**Tier 1 — incrementally load-bearing** (nonzero permutation importance
*and* the `prompt_length` feature group's removal costs accuracy,
`-0.0051`):

| Feature | Permutation importance (mean, n_repeats=30) | Single-feature balanced accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| `long_prompt_fraction_512` | 0.00282 | 0.975 |
| `long_prompt_fraction_2048` | 0.00139 | 0.998 |
| `prompt_tokens_mean` | 0.00074 | 0.982 |

**Tier 2 — individually strong classifiers via redundancy, but not
incrementally load-bearing** (zero permutation importance; removing their
whole feature group costs **0.000** accuracy — `arrival_burstiness` and
`output_length` groups are fully substitutable by the rest of the matrix):

`total_tokens_p90` (0.980), `prompt_tokens_p90` (0.977), `total_tokens_mean`
(0.972), `interarrival_p50_s` (0.965), `mean_arrival_rate_rps` (0.912) — all
strong single-feature classifiers, but removing them (individually or as a
group) does not measurably hurt the full model, because their signal is
redundant with Tier 1 and each other.

**RQ4 answer**: prompt-length distribution — specifically the fraction of
long prompts at the 512/2048-token thresholds and mean prompt length — is
the primary, non-redundant driver of source separability. Arrival-timing
structure and output length are individually predictive (workloads *are*
different along those axes too) but carry no *additional* signal once
prompt-length features are present.

### Critical ablation (`critical_ablation_summary.csv`)

Removing `total_tokens_mean`, `long_prompt_fraction_8192`, or even the top
2 Tier-1 features together, changes RF balanced accuracy by at most
**-0.0025** (28 → 26 features: 1.000 → 0.9975). Separability is broad and
not dependent on any single feature.

## Caveat (preserve in any manuscript reuse)

`prompt_tokens_*` composes differently across sources:
- **TraceLab**: `input_tokens_total` is the full cumulative context sent
  each round, including reused prefix accumulated across a growing
  agent session.
- **Azure 2024**: `ContextTokens` is also potentially multi-turn-cumulative
  per Azure's own schema.
- **BurstGPT**: `Request tokens` is a single per-request figure.

This is a genuine property of how each source's underlying workload is
architected (a single stateless API call vs. a growing agent session), not
an artifact this project's adapters introduced — and it is very likely
*part of why* prompt-length features separate sources so well. State this
explicitly alongside any prompt-length-based separability claim; do not
present it as evidence the sources' underlying conversations/tasks differ
in length, only that their *observed request-level prompt-token figures*
do, for a mix of architectural and possibly-genuine-content reasons that
this analysis cannot separate.
